Over the 30 days to August 9 your campaign spent $100.90 — about $3.36 a day. For that, 5,852 people within an eight-mile radius saw Mofongos at the top of their search results, 352 of them clicked through, and Google recorded 1,139 follow-up actions — menu views, directions requests, taps to call and other on-site engagement.

The single most valuable change we can make: your ad currently sends every click to mofongosrestaurant.com — the homepage — instead of straight to your online ordering page. Google recorded 9 order starts in this period but only 1 completed order traced back to the ad. Cutting the extra step between the click and the cart is the highest-value fix available, and it costs nothing.

What's working

Three results worth calling out

These are the parts of the campaign that are already performing well ahead of what we would expect for a neighbourhood restaurant on a small test budget.

6.02%

An unusually high click rate

Roughly one in every 17 people who see your ad clicks it. For comparison, a well-tuned restaurant search campaign typically lands between 2% and 3.5%. Your ad copy and your name are resonating.

$0.29

Visits are inexpensive

Twenty-nine cents to put a hungry person in your neighbourhood on your website. At this rate the whole month of traffic cost about the same as two entrees.

1,139

People engage once they arrive

Menu views, directions, calls and site engagement across the 30 days — more than three actions for every click. Diners who find you are genuinely interested; they just aren't reaching a checkout.

Demand pattern

When your customers are searching

Demand is firmly a lunch story. Searches build from 10am, peak hard at noon, and taper through the afternoon and evening. Your ads currently run around the clock, seven days a week.

Almost all of your traffic is on a phone — 341 of 352 clicks. Mobile searchers click your ad at 6.10%, against 4.20% on desktop. That is exactly the behaviour you want for a neighbourhood restaurant: people searching while they're out and hungry. It also means the ordering experience on a phone is where the money is won or lost.

Two patterns to act on. First, about a third of the month's spend — roughly $34 — went to people already searching for Mofongos by name. That traffic converts, but it is the traffic you were most likely to win anyway. Second, every keyword on the account is set to Google's widest matching option with no exclusions in place, which is why searches for other restaurants and other cuisines are appearing at all. Both are straightforward to tighten.

Headroom

You're being shown for 17% of the searches you could win

Google reports how much of the available search demand in your area your ad actually appeared for. The gap tells us where the ceiling is — and, usefully, whether the constraint is money or ad quality.

This is the reason the plan below leads with quality, not budget. Only 27% of the missed searches were lost because the daily budget ran out; 56% were lost because competing ads outranked yours. Raising the budget before improving ad rank would buy more of the same. Tightening keywords, adding exclusions and pointing the ad at a page that matches the search all improve rank at no extra cost — and make a later budget increase go considerably further.

What happens next

Our optimization plan

No optimization work has been carried out on this campaign since it launched in May, so everything below is available to us now. In priority order.

1

Send clicks straight to online ordering

Repoint the ad, and the sitelinks beneath it, from the homepage to your Chowly ordering page. Today a diner who clicks has to find the ordering link themselves. Nine people started an order this month and one finished — removing that extra step is the single biggest lever on this account.
High priority
2

Add exclusions to stop wasted spend

There are currently no blocked search terms on the account. We'll exclude competitor names, neighbouring cuisines that aren't yours (Salvadoran, Colombian, Ecuadorian, soul food) and the very broad "restaurants near me" style searches that rarely lead to an order. Every dollar saved goes back to diners who are ready to buy.
High priority
3

Tighten keyword matching and retire the weak ones

All 25 keywords use Google's widest match setting, and several — "caribbean food", "best puerto rican food", "puerto rican food" — carry a low quality score, which is what drives the ad-rank losses above. We'll move the proven terms to tighter matching, pause the poor performers, and free that spend for "mofongo near me" and "puerto rican food near me", which are already earning clicks at well under a dollar.
High priority
4

Match ad hours to your open hours

The campaign runs 24 hours a day. Overnight is a small leak today — 54 impressions and one click between 1am and 6am — but with demand peaking so sharply at noon, weighting the schedule toward the lunch and dinner windows puts more of the budget where the orders actually are.
Medium
5

Build out the ad extensions

Five quick links are live (View Menu, Catering, Events, About, Contact) but there is no "Order Online" link, and no callouts or highlights at all. Adding them makes your ad physically larger on the results page and gives diners a direct path to the cart — at no extra cost per click.
Medium
6

Revisit the daily budget together

At $3.30 a day you're reaching about one in six of the relevant searches near you — the demand is clearly there. Once the four changes above have had two to three weeks to take effect, we'll bring you a recommendation with the numbers to support it, rather than asking for more budget first.
Medium
